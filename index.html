    The optimisation result

    What binds: county delivery capacity, not levy revenue

    The 6,000-unit programme costs ≈ KSh 19.75B — just 27% of one year's levy. Most funded counties are stopped by their own delivery ceilings, not money. Recommended: Option C — capacity-building where capacity binds, cash where it doesn't.

    Two ways to spend the same money

    Regime A concentrates units; Regime B serves all 47. Same 6,000 units, same cost — the real rationing happens at county capacity.

    Which constraint binds? (Regime A)

    Per-county diagnosis: is each county stopped by its own delivery capacity, the national quota, or the budget?

    What the diagnosis means

      Capacity ceilings are built from county permits issued, permit approval rates, completions records, units under construction and financier-financed units — an independent supply-side measure, not the demand backlog and not the DEA score.

      The aspirational scenario

      At the official 200,000-unit target, the full 15,365-unit backlog (KSh 50.58B) is affordable within the KSh 73.2B levy, but only 1,795 units were completed in FY2024/25.

      Need vs efficiency vs allocation (cross-tier check)

      High-need, low-efficiency counties should shift between regimes — the proposal's consistency test.
