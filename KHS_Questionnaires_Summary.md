# Summary of Questionnaires – 2023/24 Kenya Housing Survey (KHS) and County Modules

## Scope

This file summarizes the **six questionnaires** in the archive `Questionnaires, technical documents folder.`. It does not cover the technical reports (Basic Report, Quality Report, Pilot Survey Report, Real Estate Survey Report, KHS Personnel Training Manual, DDI documentation).

All six are instruments of the **2023/24 Kenya Housing Survey ("Makao Fiti, Maisha Bora")**. The survey is run by the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) with the State Department for Housing and Urban Development under the Statistics Act 2006. Responses are confidential and published only in aggregate. Reference period is mostly 2019–2023, and the Household and Developers instruments also use the last 12 months, "since 1 Jan 2023" or "last week/7 days".

| # | Questionnaire | Respondent | Length | Main focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Household Module | Households | 53 pp | Demographics, dwelling, amenities, tenure, tenancy and ownership, affordability |
| 2 | Institutional (Developers, Contractors and Real Estate Agents) | Developers, contractors, estate agents | 57 pp | Projects, costs, financing, materials, property sales and rentals |
| 3 | Financiers | Lenders and housing financiers | 9 pp | Housing loans, mortgages, NPLs, barriers |
| 4 | County Government – Institutional and Regulatory Framework | County governments | 6 pp | Building approvals, planning, land, budgets |
| 5 | NEMA | National Environment Management Authority (county offices) | 4 pp | Environmental licensing of housing |
| 6 | WSSP | Water, sewerage and service providers | 6 pp | Water and sewer connections and coverage |

Questionnaires 4–6 share the same cover and a standard Section 1 (institution particulars: name, address, GPS, county, contact person, respondent) before their own module.

## 1. Household Module (53 pp)

**Structure:** identification and consent (A), roster (B), then Sections C–M, followed by a child-labour module.

- **A – Identification and survey staff:** county to ward, cluster, structure and housing unit number, household head, GPS, visit log with result codes (found, absent, refused, vacant, destroyed, etc.), and a consent script.
- **B – Household roster:**
  - Demographics: relationship to head, sex, age, marital status, religion.
  - Disability (age 5+): six functional-difficulty items (seeing, hearing, walking, remembering, self-care, communicating), each on a four-point scale.
  - Migration: county and country of birth, years in current place, reason for moving, previous residence type.
  - Education: current level, highest level completed, distance, mode, time and cost of travel to school.
  - ICT: mobile phone ownership and use, internet use, computer use (last 3 months).
  - Labour force (age 5+): work in the last 7 days, job search actions, availability, employment status.
  - Primary job: occupation, industry, employer type, hours, and distance, mode, time and cost of the commute.
- **C – Housing amenities:**
  - Water: main drinking and other-use sources, access mode, shortages, collection time and who collects, reasons for having no piped water.
  - Sanitation and hygiene: toilet type and sharing, handwashing facility.
  - Energy: electricity connection and reasons for none, main lighting and cooking energy, fuel collection time and injuries, cookstove type, time spent cooking and harm from cooking.
  - Smart and ICT technologies in the home.
  - Average monthly household expenditure and savings.
  - Adequacy: cultural fit, discrimination in accessing housing (by gender, religion, culture, marital status, social status, disability, race, tribe), perceived adequacy of the dwelling, and barriers such as rent or land cost, lack of title, loan eligibility, material cost, interest rates, income.
- **D – Dwelling unit characteristics:**
  - Type: bungalow, flat, maisonette, Swahili/compound house, shanty, manyatta, townhouse, etc.
  - Features: attached or detached, electricity, piped water, air conditioning, tenure (own, rent, no rent), rooms, bedrooms, floor area.
  - Main floor, wall and roof materials, using detailed natural, rudimentary and finished codes.
- **E – Environment and location:**
  - Neighbourhood association membership and facilities (parking, pool, CCTV, open space, guards, gym).
  - Solid-waste disposal and collection frequency, waste volume in 20 kg buckets.
  - Flood and mudslide proneness, terrain.
  - Proximity to hazards (river, swamp, quarry, dumpsite, factory, bars, airport, busy road) and noise, air and water pollution.
- **G – Transport and infrastructure:** distances to schools, police, health facility, bus stop, market and others, road type, street lighting, and which utilities (lighting, walkways, drainage, sewerage, water, roads, health, garbage, recreation) are provided and functional.
- **H – Disability and accessibility of the home:** accessibility rating, safety, lighting, wide doorways, grab bars, ramps, lever handles, elevators, alarms, reachable switches, kitchen accommodations.
- **I – Land ownership and tenure:** for each parcel: how acquired, owners, county, land category, tenure system, document type, rights to sell and bequeath, and perceived likelihood and main cause of involuntary loss of rights in 5 years.
- **J – Integrated module (age 18+):**
  - Comfort with current location, barriers to relocating, night-time safety, crime victimisation.
  - Awareness of the housing right and of building rules, perceived problems with them, the 7 m² per person minimum, and views on regulating rents, prices and interest rates.
  - Move history and reasons.
  - Awareness and uptake of Affordable Housing incentives: stamp-duty exemption for first-time buyers, 15% relief on contributions (up to KSh 9,000/month), and tax deductibility of mortgage interest (up to KSh 300,000/yr).
- **K – Tenants module:**
  - Landlord type, written agreement, tenure length, reason for choosing the area, rent, utilities included, rent changes.
  - Subletting, repair responsibility, preferred rental type and bedrooms, and willingness to pay.
  - Plans to build or buy: type, location, budget, savings and loan access, proportion saved.
  - Rent disputes: arbitration, awareness and use of the Rent Restriction Tribunal, demolitions, eviction threats.
  - Other houses owned and the reason for renting.
- **L – Owner-occupied module:** how the unit was acquired, financing (cash, loan, mortgage), lender, principal, interest, duration, repayment status, construction timeline and completeness, estimated sale and rental value, professionals used (architect, engineer, QS, surveyor and others), reasons for choosing the location, annual land rates, ground rent and service charge, improvements since 2018, subletting, demolition fears, development approvals.
- **M – Owners not occupying:** other houses owned elsewhere: location, type, bedrooms, how acquired, approvals, professionals used, use (rental, second home, holiday, unused), value, last maintenance.
- **Child labour module (ages 5–17):**
  - NB: work for pay, business and farm work.
  - NC: unpaid training and own-use production, such as fetching water, firewood and construction.
  - ND: hazardous work conditions (heavy loads, tools, chemicals, heights, night work and similar).

## 2. Institutional Questionnaire – Developers, Contractors and Real Estate Agents (57 pp)

**Routing (Q1):** residential developers → Modules 2, 3, 4; commercial developers → Modules 2, 4; real estate management → Modules 5–10.

- **Module 2 – General (developers and contractors):**
  - Economic activity and turnover (2023), employment and earnings (June 2022 and June 2023, by sex and type).
  - Establishment type, legal status, ownership structure, sex of owners and directors, year started housing development.
  - Registration with KPDA, NCA, KABCEC, KFMB, KREDRB, VRB, and reasons for non-registration.
  - Ratings of process time for land acquisition, change of user, infrastructure, financing and offtake. Time, fee and efficiency ratings for approvals from County, NEMA and NCA.
  - Contracting forms (sub-contracting, labour-only, self-build, turnkey).
  - Whether statutory professional fees are fair and whether they are negotiated: architects 6%, surveyors 3.5%, valuers 2%, estate agents 3%.
  - Awareness of alternative building technologies (stabilised soil blocks, EPS, 3D printing, precast, aluminium formwork, cross-laminated timber, recycled plastic blocks, etc.).
  - Rating of challenges (land cost, infrastructure, credit, materials cost, approvals, litigation and more) as major, minor or not a challenge.
  - Awareness and uptake of VAT exemption on affordable-housing inputs and the 15% corporation tax rate for 100+ units.
  - Construction spending 2019–2023 (new, extension, alteration, repairs), number and list of projects.
- **Module 3 – Project-specific (up to 10 projects):**
  - Location, land tenure and ownership, change of user, contractor type, client type, factors influencing location choice, contract type.
  - Timeline, percent complete, residential or mixed use, main floor, wall and roof materials, and material sourcing (local or imported, and why).
  - Green certification (EDGE, LEED), alternative technologies used and why.
  - Services availability and connection time and cost (water, sewer, electricity), design features (solar, rainwater harvesting, smart systems, broadband, amenities), water, energy and waste arrangements, disability provisions.
  - Loan attempts, denial reasons, financing sources, loan terms, cost distribution by input, housing types, build approach (one go or phased).
  - Unit counts by type and bedrooms (with or without DSQ), plinth and floor areas, sale or rent, average cost, units sold or rented.
- **Module 4 – Non-residential buildings (2019–2023):** types developed (warehouse, offices, factory, hotels, hospitals, schools, malls, parking, religious buildings and others).
- **Module 5 – Real estate agent general:** same pattern as Module 2 (turnover, employment, ownership), registration with the Estate Agents Registration Board and others, financing help offered to buyers and renters, ownership of properties dealt in.
- **Modules 6–9 – Property listings since 1 Jan 2023:**
  - Module 6, residential for sale; Module 7, residential for rent; Module 8, commercial for sale; Module 9, commercial for rent.
  - Per property: type, bedrooms, bathrooms, parking, DSQ, amenities, location, size, land size, year built, advertised price and actual price.
  - Rentals add service charge, rent changes, rent-collection software and payment mode. Commercial lettings add services included in the service charge.
- **Module 10 – Warehousing:** facility types (grain silo, general, bonded, refrigerated, storage tanks), capacity, utilised and idle capacity, 2022 and 2023 rent and sales earnings.

## 3. Financiers Questionnaire (9 pp)

Single module (S), 16 items, reference date 31 Dec 2023.

- **SE1:** type of financier (commercial bank, MFB, housing finance institution, MFI, SACCO, employer scheme, multilateral, chama, REIT, Kenya Mortgage Refinance Company, other).
- **SE2:** savings products geared to land purchase, incremental building, mortgages.
- **SE3:** loan products offered (land, construction for sale, rent or owner occupation, incremental, mortgage, graduated, rental purchase), with number, value and NPL value for each.
- **SE4–SE5:** housing NPL share of the portfolio and the days past due at which a loan is treated as non-performing (30–180).
- **SE5b:** units financed by mortgage (apartments and houses).
- **SE6:** mortgage fees (valuation, conveyancing, insurance, stamp duty, building insurance) as a percent of the loan.
- **SE7–SE8:** average 2023 mortgage interest rate and fixed-rate duration.
- **SE9:** products for youth, women, PWDs, Sharia-compliant and multi-generational lending, and the level of demand.
- **SE10–SE11:** methods to encourage timely payment, and recovery actions used in the last five years (rescheduling, auction, private sale, refinancing, receivership, notional rent).
- **SE12:** key barrier to mortgage and construction lending (unreliable title as collateral, high default, lack of long-term capital, fluctuating rates, lengthy legal and registration processes).
- **SE13–SE15:** restrictions on housing funding and their basis (regulator, board, risk policy, capital adequacy, lack of long-term funds), and any incentives to lend more.
- **SE16:** main challenge in recovering loans (lengthy legal process, mismanagement, multiple borrowing, diversion of funds, unwillingness to pay).

## 4. County Government – Institutional and Regulatory Framework (6 pp, items CG1–CG25)

- **Applications and approvals (CG1–CG7):**
  - Residential applications received in 2022 and 2023, digitisation of processes, georeferenced land records.
  - Consulted institutions (Physical Planning, NCA, Public Health, State Department for Housing, NEMA, KURA).
  - Average approval time, approvals by use type (housing, commercial, warehousing, mixed), reasons for decline (plot ratio, lack of services, zoning, unregistered professionals, unpaid rates, ownership disputes).
  - Average approval cost by floor area.
- **Capacity (CG8):** in-house professionals (architects, engineers, planners, QS, surveyors).
- **Land use and compliance (CG9–CG13):**
  - Change and extension of user criteria.
  - Completion certificates received and issued.
  - Whether Code 95 (revised building bylaws) has been ratified.
  - Main approval challenge (political interference, capacity, no approved plan, land tenure) and measures taken.
- **Informal settlements and materials (CG14–CG16):** informal settlements by council type and their share, improvement measures (infrastructure, tenure regularisation, relocation), common raw materials for foundations and walling.
- **Budget (CG17):** share of the county budget for housing development, maintenance and slum upgrading, 2019–2023.
- **Planning (CG18–CG21):** urban centres with approved Local Physical and Land Use Development Plans, reasons for non-approval, hectares planned for residential use (approved and unapproved), area planned for housing on public and private land.
- **Land release and change of user (CG22–CG24):** steps to release urban land under freehold, change and extension of user applications 2019–2023 and their direction.
- **CG25:** how development in residential zones is controlled (multi-select).

## 5. NEMA Questionnaire (4 pp, items NEMA1–NEMA9)

- NEMA1–3: housing-development licence applications received and licensed in 2022 and 2023, split into housing only and mixed use.
- NEMA4–5: factors considered when licensing or refusing (site suitability, alternatives, public participation, management plan, EIA, effluent and waste, environmental effects) and in costing a licence.
- NEMA6: average time to obtain a licence.
- NEMA7: whether the Environmental Management Plan and Audit are enforced, and why not if they are not.
- NEMA8–9: main licensing challenges (applicants not meeting requirements, patronage, expert shortages, agency conflict, late feedback) and measures taken (more staff, digital system, stakeholder education, role streamlining, etc.).

---

## 6. WSSP Questionnaire (6 pp, items WSSP1–WSSP17)

- WSSP1–4: water and sewer connection applications in 2022 and 2023, residential versus mixed use.
- WSSP5–7: sewer coverage (%), plans to extend water and sewer to residential areas, sewer cost per metre.
- WSSP8: reasons for declining connections (distance to main line, unpaid fees, no documents, land disputes, court orders).
- WSSP9–11: time to get water and sewer connections, average connection charges, and how fair tap-water distribution is ensured across low, medium and high-density areas.
- WSSP12–13: connections in informal settlements, cubic metres supplied to residential areas in 2022 and 2023, introduction of smart meters.
- WSSP14–15: delivery challenges (ageing pipes, leaks, finance, contamination, staffing, population influx) and responses.
- WSSP16–17: views on rainwater harvesting and the extent of non-residential use of treated water.

## Notes on accuracy

- The questionnaires are text-layer PDFs. I extracted them programmatically and read each in full except the Household Module, which I read in full at the section and question level but not cell by cell. The roster pages (B) and the long code lists (education levels, county codes, occupation and industry codes) are summarized rather than reproduced.
- Question numbering follows the source. In some sections, such as the NEMA, WSSP and County sheets, the source's layout is partly garbled in extraction, so a few details of response options are paraphrased rather than exact.
- Items such as the professional-fee percentages (6%, 3.5%, 2%, 3%) and the incentive amounts (KSh 9,000/month, KSh 300,000/yr) appear exactly as printed in the questionnaires, not as verified current law.
